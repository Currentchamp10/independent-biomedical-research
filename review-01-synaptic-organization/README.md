# Review 01 — Nanoscale Synaptic Organization and Activity-Dependent Plasticity

## Central Question

How do glutamate receptor trafficking and nanoscale organization interact with intracellular signalling and structural remodelling to regulate synaptic plasticity?

## Overview

This review examines the molecular and cellular mechanisms through which excitatory synapses undergo activity-dependent changes in strength. Particular attention is given to AMPA- and NMDA-type glutamate receptors, their organization within the postsynaptic membrane, receptor trafficking and anchoring, CaMKII signalling, actin-dependent structural plasticity, and the influence of the membrane environment on synaptic function.

## Main Areas of Analysis

* Glutamate receptor organization and nanoscale receptor domains
* AMPA receptor trafficking, mobility, anchoring, and endocytosis
* NMDA receptor organization and activity-dependent signalling
* CaMKII-dependent synaptic plasticity
* Actin-mediated dendritic spine remodelling
* Membrane lipid regulation of receptor organization and function
* Experimental approaches for studying synaptic organization

## Experimental Approaches Discussed

The review considers evidence obtained using:

* Super-resolution microscopy
* Confocal microscopy
* Live-cell imaging
* Single-particle tracking
* Electrophysiology
* Molecular perturbation
* Quantitative image analysis

## Key Research Question

How are receptor organization, membrane properties, intracellular signalling, and cytoskeletal remodelling coordinated to convert neuronal activity into persistent changes in synaptic function?
